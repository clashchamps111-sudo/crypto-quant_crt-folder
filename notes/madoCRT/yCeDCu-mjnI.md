### 01:50–04:34 What CRT actually is
- **M-1 [P] CANDLE = RANGE:** "Every single candle you see on a chart, whether it's a daily candle, a 4-hour candle, a 1-hour candle, or any other time frame, is not just a random shape. It is a **range, a time-based range**."
- Zoom-in property: inside any candle "you see a smaller range forming with a clear high and a clear low."
- **M-2 [P] THE THREE ELEMENTS:** only 3 things needed: "Liquidity, time, price... All three of these elements are perfectly wrapped into one system, CRT."
- **M-3 [P] LIQUIDITY:** "The market always moves from liquidity to liquidity. Either it takes buyside liquidity and then runs toward sellside liquidity, or it does the opposite."
- **M-4 [P] H/L = LIQUIDITY POOLS:** "The candle range or CRT high represents buyside liquidity and the candle range low represents sellside liquidity."
- **M-5 [P] TIME:** every candle "forms within a specific time window... each one has a defined open and close. That open and close represent a time-based range. The market is not just moving in price. It is moving within time."
- **M-6 [P] PRICE:** "The candle itself is the price range from high to low, from open to close. That is your structure... what the market is giving you directly without any indicators."
- **M-7 [P] BACKBONE FORMULA:** "liquidity plus time plus price is equals to time-based ranges. This is the backbone of the entire CRT system."

### 04:34–05:33 Typical (conventional) ranges — why CRT supersedes them
- Problem 1: "the highs and lows are inconsistent. It's never clear which high or which low actually matters. Everyone marks them differently."
- Problem 2: "there is no fixed opening and closing time. With typical ranges, you never truly know where the range starts or where it ends. It's subjective. Two traders can look at the same chart and draw two completely different ranges."
- Problem 3: "there are no fixed targets... no clear objectives or clear liquidity draws. You're not trading with precision. You're just guessing."
- CRT answers all three: fixed H/L, fixed open/close times, fixed targets.

### 05:33–08:01 Candle ranges (mechanics)
- "Every single candle you see on a chart is a range. Whether it's a monthly candle, a daily candle, or a 1-hour candle, they all have the exact same structure. Open, high, low, close."
- **M-8 [P] FRACTALITY:** "The only real difference is speed. A monthly candle simply moves slower... this is the true meaning of fractality. Whatever you see happening on a higher time frame will also happen on a lower time frame. The structure stays the same, only the speed changes."
- Candle ranges = objective: "fixed highs and fixed lows, clear reference points... a fixed opening time and a fixed closing time."
- **M-9 [P] FIXED TARGETS:** "candle ranges also give you fixed targets. Every candle has a high and a low. And those highs and lows are liquidity pools. The market will either raid one side or the other. And that behavior is what gives us our entries."
- **M-10 [P] THE TWO POSSIBILITIES:** "With every candle range there are only two possibilities."
  1. "TRULY broken: price breaks above the CRT high or below the CRT low **and continues in that same direction**."
  2. "FALSELY broken (turtle soup): price raids one side of the range... then reverses and runs to the other side of the range." — "the second one is the most important one. This is the foundation of CRT."
- **M-11 [P] CORE REVERSAL MECHANIC:** "price opens, soup one side of the range, then declines or expands toward the opposite side, eventually targeting the range low or the range high." (Sweep -> reversal to opposite boundary.)
